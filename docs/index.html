Research2020

Biological Soil Crusts for Reclamation of Mine Tailings

What if the best way to heal a mine is to let the ground grow its own armor?

Biological soil crusts are the living skin of moss, lichen, and microbes that forms on dry ground, and they can hold stressed soil against wind and water erosion. This applied research project, my MSc thesis work, asked how much they can do on mine tailings facilities in Canada, a question that was new to the country at the time.

I did field work across four mine sites, pairing GIS spatial sampling and interpolation with lab and statistical analysis to see what the crusts were made of and what they did. Then I built experimental plots to test which amendments help a crust get established. The result is a quantified picture of the reclamation effects, plus guidelines for the next round of biocrust research against erosion.

The project was run with Stantec and Taseko | Gibraltar Mine, backed by $15,000 in grant funding, and it contributed to Gibraltar winning the Jake McDonald Annual Award for Metal Mine Reclamation. It's also fully reproducible: the appendix includes the R script that runs every hypothesis test and produces every data product.

Research2018

Salamander Habitat Suitability Study

Where would you look for a salamander that isn't there yet?

That's the puzzle behind this study: could terrestrial salamanders be reintroduced to a reclaimed, naturalized land parcel in Cobourg, Ontario, and if so, where would they thrive? I sampled 30 hectares for salamanders, vernal pools, water quality, and soil quality, and used the salamanders native to the surrounding area to set a habitat suitability goal.

Then I put the parcel through a multi-variable habitat model that combined climate modelling, vernal pool hydrology, and the distribution of woody debris on the ground. The output is a suitability map for each species, built in partnership with Trent Research Centre and Northumberland Land Trust. For the Lorie Lawson education center on the land, it doubles as a heat map: once salamanders arrive, it shows visitors where they're most likely to spot one.

Mapping2017

Watershed Management Plan for the Grijalva River Watershed

What would a river basin look like if it were planned around what the land can actually do?

This capstone project answers that for the Grijalva watershed in Tabasco and Chiapas, Mexico. I projected the watershed's environmental landscape and compared it against the land-use patterns already on the ground. It began with sketching the watershed on tracing paper and ended with climate models for agriculture, with hundreds of hours in between.

The professor dangled a trip to Mexico for the best project in the class. That trip never came to be, but a government delegation traveled over instead, and we presented the plan to them.

Modelling and Code2026

GeoSteerML: Predicting Wellbore Geology from Gamma Ray Logs

Can a single gamma ray log tell you where a horizontal well sits in the rock?

GeoSteerML is a prototype built around the parameters of the ROGII Wellbore Geology Prediction competition on Kaggle. I built a full machine learning pipeline that estimates True Vertical Thickness (TVT) along horizontal wells using nothing but Gamma Ray readings.

The pipeline aligns each well geologically against typewell references, processes the signal at multiple scales, and uses leakage-aware feature engineering to keep the results honest. LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost models are then trained and evaluated with well-grouped cross-validation. I never submitted it to the competition; it served as a guideline for development.

Research2020

There are NOT plenty of fish in the sea

There are plenty of fish in the sea, right?

For Map The Systems 2020, we ran a local qualitative study of the health and education of fisheries in the Lower Mainland, BC. What we found is a strong disconnect between the fisheries industry, the ecology, and the consumers who buy the fish. We used a number of qualitative research techniques to get there, and the poster that came out of it is the best way to see the results.
